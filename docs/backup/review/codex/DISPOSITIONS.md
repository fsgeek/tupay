# Dispositions of Codex's review of backup contract draft 3

**Reviewer:** Codex, run by Tony, independently of Qwen's findings (its own statement). Relayed by Tony on 2026-11-11, and kept verbatim in `review.md` beside this file. It reviewed the contract's text, not the implementation.

**Disposed by** the twelfth steward, 2026-11-11. **Eight findings: all accepted.** Draft 4 carries them.

| # | Finding | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Faithful" does not require completeness: a manifest can describe an incomplete snapshot. "Every ref resolves" does not show one simultaneous state. | **Accepted.** New B1.17 (complete against a source inventory taken at the start of the run, with creation, deletion and renaming during the run each treated explicitly). The repository is bundled from one ref list read once, and the witness compares the bundle's refs with that list. |
| 2 | T7b is stored unencrypted, but B1.10 requires every stored object to be ciphertext. And a ciphertext match shows the bytes are preserved, not that decryption or restore works. | **Accepted. The contradiction came from the steward's fix for Qwen's finding 1.** Resolved by the scope decision below. In every version, T11's evidence is a passing T10; a T8 newer than it shows only that the checked bytes are unchanged. |
| 3 | A restore check that fails has no defined mark, notice or pruning rule. | **Accepted.** A new mark, *restore-failed*: a notice under B1.16, and pruning never removes the newest snapshot that is not *restore-failed*. |
| 4 | The blanket H makes promises stronger than the exceptions support: a crash during the restore check; BF1.12 against B1.6; box suspension as a cell; S's key loss under B1.9. | **Accepted.** Draft 4 separates **service obligations** (what the service does or never does) from **environmental losses** and **availability conditions**, and the effect table is filled in by that distinction. Specifically: plaintext left by a crash is removed at the next start, within *D*; B1.6's "nothing removes" becomes "the service removes nothing except by pruning"; BF1.14, a host suspended, is a new cell; S's key loss is moot under the scope decision. |
| 5 | Status-record recovery is incomplete: the case where only R's copy is lost, obligations erased when both copies are lost, and sequence-number recovery. | **Accepted.** Sequence numbers recover as max(R's snapshot names, S's record) + 1. A record that both copies lost is rebuilt by re-verifying and restore-checking every snapshot present, within a deadline *Rb*. During the rebuild, pruning is frozen, so that no known obligation is lost. |
| 6 | "Within *T* … measured by age" mixes two quantities. Re-verification covers only the retained span, while B1.3 protects every unpruned snapshot. | **Accepted.** The windows are defined by age alone. B1.12 covers every unpruned snapshot. |
| 7 | The 27 h bound is conditional (another successful run; the threshold crossed; delivery available). *T* should carry those conditions. | **Accepted.** Draft 4 states *Dmax* and *T* with their conditions. Under BF1.2 and BF1.4, no detection bound holds, and the contract says so. |
| 8 | The credential assumption is checked at the wrong boundary (at push, not at backup), and its detector is not reproducible. | **Accepted.** The detector is `scripts/steward/secrets-scan.sh`, a fixed pattern set committed with its own mutation pair. Every run scans the commits new since the previous snapshot, and a hit fails the run at stage *no-credentials*. |
| — | **The larger point:** state what Tony can recover first. | **Accepted, and done first:** `docs/backup/recovery-account.md`, which describes today's state, checked against the machines. Draft 4 opens with it, and every clause names which row of the account it improves. |

## Scope decision (the steward's; not a finding)
**Encryption at rest moves out of v1 and into v2, as a planned contract change.**
- **Why:** encryption caused both reviews' blocking manifest findings (Qwen 1, Codex 2), and it carries five ids (B1.10, B1.14, BF1.11, AI1.6, EX1.4). Its key custody is being redesigned for the whole house before the Fedora move (plaza seq 246: "better key management before the move", which Tony is working on with Tessera). Writing tupay's key clauses now would bind them to a custody design that is about to change.
- **What it costs:** nothing against today. `backup.sh` is plaintext now, so v1 without encryption is no regression. It is stated as EX1.7, with a review date of 2026-12-01.
- **What it buys:** a **new authority locus, the key holder, arriving as a contract change** after v1 has artifacts. That is the seed's adapt-or-regenerate question at the scale X3 tested, on a service tupay runs. Tony called the question a hyperplane of which only one side had been seen. This change is planned by tupay, so it is weaker than one the adversary times, and the registration has to say so.
- **What it retires:** B1.10, B1.14, BF1.11, AI1.6 and EX1.4 are **retired in v1**, never reused, and v2 states them afresh with new ids. T7a and T7b merge into one manifest (T7c).
