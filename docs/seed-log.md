## 2026-10-20: idea for later, not in scope (Tony, from a ChatGPT conversation)
Contracts that trace indeterminacy against performance. Some effects cannot absorb rollback (they have crossed an event horizon); some can. A contract that marks which effects may be issued speculatively, which observation collapses the uncertainty, and where the horizon is, would let a system speculate where rollback is improbable and pay for it only when it happens. Prior art: externally synchronous I/O (Nightingale et al., 2006: output commit is the horizon), Speculative Paxos, Zyzzyva. In tupay's v1 contract, C1.3's ack is the one horizon clause, and the fence ledger is the commit point that collapses the holder's indeterminacy. X1 (fenced reads) is where an unfenced read escapes the horizon with no ledger to reject it. Not to be started before the change experiments. Recorded so it is not lost.

## 2026-10-20: the indeterminacy idea, refined (ChatGPT via Tony; steward's reading)
Correction accepted: Speculator (SOSP '05) gates output on correctness; Rethink the Sync (OSDI '06) gates it on durability. The previous entry conflated them. Adopted as a candidate clause property: where H is the externalization point and C the event that resolves whether the authority was valid, H before C is unrecoverable speculation unless the clause names a compensation boundary; C before H may speculate freely inside. Candidate clause vocabulary for a later contract version: provisional effect, validation event, event horizon, compensation boundary; a clause as (allowed speculation, validation predicate, externalization boundary), with a failure-model cell supplying when validation can fail. Steward's observation: X1 (fenced reads) already is "move the horizon across the resolution point": an unfenced read has H before C with no ledger to reject it; fencing moves C in front of H. That wording goes into X1's pre-registration. Nothing is changed in contract v1.

## 2026-10-21: the routing question is a configuration question (Tony, ChatGPT via Tony; steward's decisions)
Tony's counsel on X2's first residue: request routing is at heart a configuration question, and configuration is where "who can grant" is defined, which makes it part of the authority problem (the authority project's HotOS observation), not an afterthought. Viewstamped Replication (Oki and Liskov, 1988) tolerates two primaries that both believe they are active; the quorum, not the node's belief, decides whose authority is real, which raises the question of how a quorum is defined (eidolon: quorum shape, not majority). Tony also offered, as a thought, that tessera's use of DNSSEC signing keys suggests DNS as an existing, robust location service for finding and verifying authority. ChatGPT, via Tony, for consideration: quorum intersection protects decisions within a configuration, not between configurations (VR Revisited, Liskov and Cowling 2012: reconfiguration committed through the old group, epoch-numbered); routing splits into discovery (finding someone who can act) and authorization (that someone being entitled to act), with authority transfer underneath both; C1.4 should state availability under a stable configuration, and the transfer of authority between configurations deserves its own experiment.
Steward's decisions, with reasons:
1. **C1.4 is not changed.** Its premise is stated in terms of the environment (who is up and connected), not of the protocol's internal roles. A premise that said "connected to the node that can grant" would put the implementation's notion of leader into the contract, which is exactly the authority question a contract must not presuppose. Read literally under failure model v2 (Rule L3), it already promises discovery under a stable configuration. X2's H8/H9 failures are therefore artifact defects, not contract ambiguity, and the first X2 residue is reclassified accordingly. Fixing them is an ordinary adaptation round, logged for cost, after the workflow fixes; H7 to H10 are promoted to shared schedules for it, since every author's checkout of the result has seen them.
2. **X3 becomes reconfiguration, not delegated tokens.** Both are new authority loci. Reconfiguration is the one the evidence points at: X2's mechanism was a question of who can grant, the authority project's thesis is the transfer of authority, and under a changing configuration "which leader?" stops having one answer. Delegated tokens move to X4. In tupay's terms the transfer invariant is C1.1 across configurations (no token issued under one configuration may fail to exceed a token issued under an earlier one) plus the fence; the contract change for X3 will add configuration epochs and say what a client may assume about the configuration it reaches. The standing prediction for X3 (regeneration wins a new locus) now rests on X2-11, not on the seed's original basis.
3. **DNSSEC is recorded as a discovery hypothesis, not adopted.** It adds a trust root outside the system and belongs to the discovery dimension, which X3 should name but need not solve. A later experiment could test it.
4. **Quorum definition stays majority in X3.** Changing the quorum system (eidolon's crumbling walls, flexible quorums) is a configuration parameter; one change per experiment.
Grok's CLI (`grok` 1.0.62) is available; it will take some of the roles Fable now holds alone (ratings or hold-outs), so that no single family authors the hold-outs, reviews the registration and rates the measures.
