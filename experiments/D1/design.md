# D1: dependency contracts of Raft, searched without the steward's hand (design, draft 3)

Steward's design (the seventh steward, Claude Opus 5.5), 2026-10-25. Draft 1 (`ad3d784`) preceded the source's fetch; draft 2 (`53e2141`) added a merge and an order rule before any enumeration existed; draft 3 applies Fable's adversarial review (`design-review/fable-1.md`; dispositions in the log, 2026-10-25, "D1 design reviewed") and is still before any enumeration. Every rule that phase 0's output could tempt is fixed here; the prereg for phase 2 may restate these rules but not change them, and any later change is an additive amendment that says whether it was made after seeing a list.

Source: `source/OngaroPhD.pdf` (Diego Ongaro, *Consensus: Bridging Theory and Practice*, Stanford, August 2014, CC licensed), fetched from web.stanford.edu/~ouster, sha256 `c9b8a814…d9d0b6`; text by `pdftotext -layout`, sha256 `ff6998de…4f525a`.

## Question
Pointed at the service contracts a consensus protocol relies on from its environment, does this project's workflow (contract and failure model first; a formal model and an implementation-side harness written by different model families; paired tests with mutation pairs; an observability layer) find a violation that breaks a real, widely used Raft implementation and that nobody has reported?

## What "blind" means here, and what it does not
Tony Mason (project lead), 2026-10-25: evidence found by other means and then credited to the workflow is theater. The two acceptable forms are a genuine bug the workflow discovers that had not been discovered, or a service that survives third-party judges the project does not control.
**The claim this design can support is "steward-blind and search-free", not "blind".** No step that chooses where to look is taken by the steward, and no search for prior reports happens before a finding; but the enumerating models were trained on the literature, and their ratings may be compressed recollection of reported bugs. That contamination is **measured** (phase 3 searches every tested cell, not only findings), not asserted absent. The commit chain (every commit OTS-stamped) evidences the procedure's order, not the absence of prior knowledge.
**The steward's residue, recorded before this design:** the 2015 single-server membership-change defect and its fix; the general argument that lease-based reads are only as safe as their clock assumptions; and the twelve entries of `docs/steward/candidates-2026-10-23.md` (committed `376bdf8`, 2026-10-23), etcd-io/raft#166 among them. **Draft 2's brief leaked part of it**: it listed Raft's promises as Figure 3.2's properties minus Leader Append-Only plus "linearizable reads where the text claims them", the one area of the residue the text ties to a clock assumption. Corrected in brief v2 before any enumeration (log, "D1 design reviewed").

## Phase 0: enumeration
- **Enumerators:** two families (Codex, Grok), **two independent runs each** (four enumerations), each a fresh instance in `scripts/steward/author-sandbox.sh` (no web: the sandbox's only route out is a proxy that allows the harness's own API host).
- **Checkout:** a separate repository per run, no history, containing exactly `AGENTS.md`, `BRIEF.md` (= `brief-enumerate.md`), `source/OngaroPhD.pdf`, `source/OngaroPhD.txt`. Its `find` listing with sha256s is committed in `phase0/manifests/` before launch; `sandbox-check.sh` passes on it and its mutation pair fails, before launch. The enumerator's report includes its own `ls -R` and `git status`.
- **Output:** `enumeration.md` in the format the brief fixes; the steward copies it, unchanged, to `phase0/<family>-<run>/enumeration.md` with the transcript, and commits it.
- **Quotation check** (`scripts/steward/d1-quote-check.py`, committed with its mutation pair before phase 0): every `source` quotation is matched against the text after normalisation (whitespace, line-break hyphenation, quote marks; a quotation broken by a page header or figure residue counts if it splits into two pieces each found within 600 characters of each other). Unverified entries are kept and ranked last by rule.

## Phase 0: predictions and abort criteria (pre-registered)
- **P0-1** Each enumeration lists between 15 and 60 contracts. p = 0.8.
- **P0-2** At least 90% of each enumeration's quotations verify. p = 0.7. **Abort:** an enumeration below 70% verified is re-run once with a fresh instance and both are reported; if the second is also below 70%, that family's lists are kept, reported, and ranked last as a whole.
- **P0-3** The two runs of one family agree on the top three ordered cells (by the rule below, on that family's list alone) in at most one of the three. p = 0.6. (A stability measure: if the top changes between runs, selection by rule is partly selection by sampling, and this is reported beside any finding.)
- **P0-4** At least one enumeration's Recognised section names a reported bug. p = 0.85.
- **Abort for the whole design:** fewer than 10 merged cells, or no merged cell with `silent` at least 3.

## Phase 1: merge and order, by rule
- **Recognised sections are removed before anything else reads the lists**, committed separately (`phase0/recognised.md`), and passed to no later step but phase 3's contamination scoring.
- **Merge:** two independent instances of a third family (Gemini via `agy`), tools disabled and web unavailable (how is settled and checked before phase 1, not before phase 0), each given only the four lists without Recognised sections and without this rule. Each outputs a mapping from every `E` id to one or more merged contracts (one `E` may map to several when it is broader than another list's entries). **A merge is accepted only where both instances produce it;** an entry they disagree on stays its own cell. Every merged contract is a cell `D<n>`; ids are never reused.
- **Order** (total, fixed now):
  1. `silent`, as the mean over the cell's member entries of each entry's **percentile rank within its own enumeration** (so a family that rates everything 5 does not dominate), descending. Sensitivity analysis (reported, not used): the maximum raw rating.
  2. `detectable`: `no`, then `partly`, then `yes`; a cell takes the most common value among its entries, ties to the less detectable.
  3. Earliest source: the minimum, over member entries, of the cited section as a numeric tuple (`6.4.1` = (6, 4, 1); a figure maps to the section containing it; front matter and unnumbered text = (0)).
  4. Remaining ties: by the NIST Randomness Beacon pulse first published after the phase 1 merge is committed: cells sorted by sha256(pulse outputValue ‖ cell id).
  Entries with unverified quotations come after every verified one, in the same order among themselves.
- The steward does not add, drop, merge or reorder cells. Draft 2's tier "found by one family before both" is dropped (Fable 9: it made merge granularity the ranking lever and favoured unverified single-finder cells).

## Phase 2: the workflow, on the first k cells
- **k = 3.** The first cell is a **pilot** with its own additive prereg; cells 2 and 3 follow under the main one.
- **Not applicable** iff the target, in its default configuration, has no code path that depends on the contract; the evidence is a cited code location, judged by a fresh instance of a family that is not the harness author's; the next cell is taken, and skipped cells are reported.
- **Target**, fixed now: one of **etcd-io/raft, hashicorp/raft, tikv/raft-rs** (the three standalone Raft libraries the steward could name from memory as most embedded in production; a choice made with the residue, and named as such), picked by the NIST beacon pulse first published after the phase 2 prereg is committed: outputValue (as an integer) mod 3, in that order. **Boundary:** the library at its latest release tag on the prereg's date, in its documented default configuration, driven by a harness the implementation-side author writes; a cell whose depended-on service the library does not touch directly (a wall clock, in a tick-driven library) is tested where that service enters the library's interface (ticks, the storage interface, the transport), and the prereg says so per cell.
- **Authors:** model and harness by different families, both in no-web sandboxes; the harness author's first commit is the injection design for the cell, written from the cell alone, **before reading the target's own test directories**. The target's own fault tooling (failpoints, robustness suites, its TLA+ specifications) is not used; if a harness author proposes it, the steward refuses and logs the proposal.
- **Inputs to phase 2:** the contract (`contract.md`, written before phase 0 from the text by rule) and the failure-model cells' contract, violation, at-risk, detectable, silent and source fields only. No Recognised section, no E-to-D mapping, no rating disagreements.
- **Controls** (in the phase 2 prereg; the known-answer bug is chosen and sealed before phase 2 by a fresh instance of a family that is neither author's, from a target pre-fix release, and unsealed after the blind run): a **known-answer** run (does the workflow find a sealed, reported environment-contract bug in a pre-fix release?); a **placebo** cell (a contract the target demonstrably defends; expected: no finding); budget per cell in hours and runs, with an abandon rule.

## Phase 3: finding, then search
- **Search over every tested cell, not only findings**, by a fresh instance that does not hold the steward's residue, with registered venues (the three targets' trackers and mailing lists, Jepsen analyses, the raft-dev list, DBLP keyword search) and query templates (in the phase 2 prereg). Reported: per cell, prior reports found or none; the fraction of the k cells with a prior public report.
- **Contaminated rediscovery:** any finding that matches an entry of the steward's residue (above) or a line of any Recognised section is scored as a known-answer outcome, **not** as evidence for the Question.
- **Documented precondition:** if the target's own documentation states the contract as an operator requirement, the finding is scored separately as "documented precondition" (the "you broke the precondition" dismissal, anticipated).
- **Disclosure:** a novel finding goes to the target's maintainers with its reproduction; response categories (fixed / acknowledged / known / documented, won't fix / no response by 30 days) are scored as registered in the phase 2 prereg. The commit order from phase 0 to the finding is published with it.

## What this does not claim
A violation found under a broken assumption can be dismissed as "you broke the precondition". The result that matters is a contract real deployments violate silently and the implementation never checks (Tony's OSR FSDK example: a per-core cache sized for 64 logical CPUs, broken years later when Windows added processor groups and a customer ran a 36-core part). Whether a cell is of that kind is reported from the ratings, the documentation check and the maintainers' response, not asserted by the steward.
