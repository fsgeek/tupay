Registered 2026-10-21 (UTC) by the second steward (Claude Opus 5.5), before any arm starts. Template: `experiments/X1-prereg.md`, whose scoring rule is kept; every departure from X1 is listed under "Departures from X1" with its reason. ## The change
`failure-model/v1.md` → `failure-model/v2.md`: links between ordered pairs of processes; "connected" defined as both directions of the direct link up; two cells, **F2.1** partial partition and **F2.2** simplex link failure; a heal restores every link. No cell retired, no locus added, contract unchanged (`contract/v2.md`). Interfaces: schedule format v4 addendum (`cut`, `drop`, rules L1 to L3, E1 extended to reads, E3 the bound, T3 restated), observe schema v4 addendum (`links` on `Fault` records; grants and retransmission). Pairs: `tests/pairs-v3.md`, self-contained, adds P19 to P30 (each clause of contract v2 × each new cell). Twelve new shared schedules `testbed/schedules/P19.yaml` to `P30.yaml`, written by the steward; thirty-one shared schedules in all (P1 to P30 and `false-suspicion-v1`).

Under the hereditary fence the change invalidates: every element that represents connectivity (it derives from F1.2's sides, and must now derive from F2.x's links as well); every element that derives from the format's action list; and every C1.4 element whose reading of "connected" assumed transitivity. It invalidates nothing tagged only with F1.1, F1.3 or a safety clause, unless that element also assumes how messages are lost.

In the seed's terms, X2 is the first test of step 4 of the workflow: a failure model revision that must percolate.

## Why this change may not be the parameter change the seed calls it
The seed's standing prediction classes X2 with X1: a parameter change, adaptation wins. The steward's doubt, registered before any outcome: under F2.1 and F2.2, C1.4's premise and the grantor's progress mechanism meet cases that bipartitions never produced. A follower that cannot hear the grantor but can reach a node that can (P25), and a grantor that is heard by a quorum it cannot hear (P26), are the textbook stalls of leader-based protocols without pre-vote and quorum checks. If either artifact's grantor must change how it elects, steps down or checks its quorum, the change reaches the structure of the artifact, not only its environment. The criterion is prediction X2-11 below. If X2-11 holds, X2 is reclassified as locus-like for the purpose of X3's standing prediction, and the seed's basis for that prediction ("a parameter change keeps the artifacts' structure") is recorded as falsified for this system. If it fails, the seed's classification stands.

## Liveness: the rules fixed before any run
- **Fairness (model side).** Fairness may be assumed only on an action whose enablement depends on the acting process's own state and on messages delivered over links that are up; never on an action that requires having heard from a peer across a down link. A model-side C1.4 PASS that rests on any other fairness is scored as not PASS and reported as a divergence with locus model.
- **The bound.** Format Rule E3: reaching `bound` before the end is `UNEXPLAINED bound`, never PASS, and is FAIL C1.4 only when a liveness check witnesses it.
- **One mechanism, scored once.** Six of the twelve new schedules need a grant issued while an F2.x fault is in force: P25 and P26 (the liveness pairs), P19 (P25's fault plus a second grant), P20 (P26's fault plus a second grant), P21 and P27 (a requester that can reach a deposed grantor must find one that can grant). If a side stalls on any of these, the judge names the mechanism. A stall is scored once, as one item under 1(e), per distinct mechanism; the other schedules in its group that stall by the same mechanism are reported as **unreached**, not FAIL, in 1(a), and their mutations as **unassessable** in 1(b). The groups: {P25, P19}, {P26, P20}, {P21, P27}. A stall in one group's schedule by another group's mechanism counts under the mechanism.
- **The bound and retries.** Rule L3 makes a client remain a requester after a lost request, so artifacts retry, and a side that records each retry as a new attempt reaches `bound: {events: 200}` sooner than one that does not. An `UNEXPLAINED bound` is not PASS, is reported separately, and goes to the judge with the trace; if the judge attributes it to the bound rather than to a stall, it is scored as **unreached**, not as a failure. (Raised by the hold-out author's second review.)
- **Unit of failure.** A model side fails a schedule if TLC reports a violation. An implementation side fails a schedule if any of its three seeds fails; the per-seed count is reported beside it. Comparisons between sides count schedules, not seeds.

## Measures (fixed here)
Per arm, per side, at the first steward run after the arm's merge, before any fix:
1. **Correctness.** (a) Shared schedules: PASS count of 31 (model) and 31 (impl, a schedule passing only if all three seeds pass; per-seed counts reported), with unreached schedules reported separately. (b) Mutations: count of 30 that fail on their pair's shared schedule; unassessable mutations reported separately. (c) Hold-out: PASS count on H7 to H10, hashes in `experiments/X2/holdout/SHA256SUMS`, never shown to any author. (d) Provenance: untraceable decisions under `observe/provenance_check.py`. (e) **Stall mechanisms:** the number of distinct C1.4 stall mechanisms, as named by the judge, and whether any is a deposed or unheard leader blocking progress (the class of M1's D1 and X1's D4).
2. **Divergence count** between an arm's two sides at first run, on the shared set and separately on the hold-out set; verdicts by a third-family judge (Gemini through `agy`, as in X1), packet inlined, never the steward's hypothesis.
3. **Cost.** Tokens and wall-clock per author (harness-reported), both, per side. For arm A, also split at the author's first commit after its predictions commit: before (reading, predicting) and after (adapting). The standing prediction is scored on **total** cost, since reading is what adaptation costs a newcomer; the after-split is reported beside it. Also: steward wall-clock; Fable advisor tokens (hold-outs, review, rating), charged to the steward; decisions asked of Tony; interruptions of Tony.
4. **Residue**, found by a fixed procedure, not by reading alone: (i) every file in the arm's diff that mentions `cut`, `drop`, `link`, `F2.1` or `F2.2` in code must carry an F2.x tag; each that does not is one residue; (ii) every tag that names a list of pairs or schedules must match the list the element actually runs; each mismatch is one residue (the X1 kind); (iii) tags naming a retired id. Then a read of the diff for anything the procedure missed, reported separately.
5. **What regeneration discards**, reported not scored, as X1's measure 5.
6. **Propagation fraction (arm A only).** Unit: (commit, file) pair in the adapting author's commits after its predictions commit, excluding README, predictions and results files, and files the artifact's own scripts generate (run outputs, observe records, evidence logs, generated mutation copies such as `model/mutations/*/Lease.tla`); the generator is rated instead. A pair is **conceptual** if any hunk in it encodes a reading of F2.x, L1 to L3 or E3, or chooses between behaviours the inputs permit; otherwise **propagation** (renames, threading a value through, carrying a new field unchanged). Fraction = propagation pairs / all pairs. Rated independently by the steward and by a Fable instance from the diffs with **commit messages stripped**; both counts and Cohen's kappa reported. Baseline: X1 arm A's adapting commits, rated by the same two raters with the same procedure before any X2 arm A commit is rated.
7. **Interpretations contradicted (arm A only).** Denominator, counted at registration: model README, 37 entries (21 numbered in §"Interpretations", R2.1 to R2.5, R2.1-a, R2.1-b, X1.1 to X1.8, V22.1); impl README, 66 numbered entries (1 to 66). Each is classed from the adapted artifact and diff: **contradicted** iff the statement, as written, is false of the adapted artifact (some situation it covered is now handled otherwise); **extended** iff it remains true of every situation it covered and the artifact now also covers new ones; **unchanged** otherwise. Rated independently by the steward and by a Fable instance; disagreements are reported, and resolved in favour of the diff, not of the author's statement. The adapting author's own statements are reported beside the classification. Pre-listed as **at risk** before any outcome: model README §"The P10 fix" (`Join`, "can reach"), §"Model summary" grantor and environment bullets, §"TLC bounds" `MaxTerm`; impl interpretations 11, 19, 25, 35, 53. Contradictions outside this list are reported as such.

## Scoring rule for the standing prediction (unchanged from X1, made explicit)
"Adaptation wins" means: arm A's correctness on 1(a) to 1(e) is at least arm R's on every item, and arm A's author cost is lower in both tokens and wall-clock on both sides. If arm R is more correct on any item, regeneration wins that item regardless of cost. A mixed result is reported as mixed and scores X2-1 as **not confirmed**.

## Predictions

- **X2-2.** No unmutated side of any scored arm violates a safety clause (C1.1 to C1.3, C2.1, C2.2) on any shared or hold-out schedule. p = 0.85. Fencing should not care how messages are lost.

Conditional on that, model sides fail at least as many of {P25, P26} as implementation sides, counted over the four sides. p = 0.6. - **X2-4.** At least one divergence on the shared set in at least one scored arm. p = 0.75.
- **X2-5.** Arm A's model side has at least one contradicted interpretation (measure 7, lower of the two raters' counts); arm A's implementation side has none. p = 0.45 for the conjunction.
- **X2-6.** The interpretation-debt hypothesis: on any side where arm A has at least one contradicted interpretation, arm A's total token cost as a fraction of arm R's on that side exceeds X1's fraction on that side (model 42k/197k ≈ 0.21; impl 110k/127.5k ≈ 0.86). **Control:** if a side with zero contradictions also exceeds its X1 fraction, Departure 1 explains the rise and X2-6 is scored **null**, not confirmed. - **X2-7.** Arm A's implementation-side propagation fraction (measure 6) exceeds X1 arm A's implementation-side fraction. Scored on the **lower** of the two raters' X2 fractions against the higher of their X1 fractions. p = 0.5. Disclosure: the steward rated the X1 baseline (model 0.60, impl 0.64; `tmp/m6-baseline/steward-ratings.txt`, committed with the result) before this file was committed; the prediction and its probability were written before that rating and were not changed.
- **X2-8.** At least one hold-out schedule produces a FAIL or a divergence in at least one scored arm. p = 0.7 (at least X2-10's).
- **X2-9.** Measure 4's procedure finds at least one residue on at least one arm A side; it finds none on either arm R side. p = 0.5.

- **X2-11.** On at least one arm A side, the grantor's election, step-down or quorum-check rule is modified, and the modification is rated conceptual by both raters under measure 6. p = 0.6.

## Input review before registration
The hold-out author was also asked to find defects in the inputs. It listed thirteen. Disposition, decided before any arm started and so not outcome-informed:
- **Fixed in the inputs** (additive text in `failure-model/v2.md`, format v4 addendum, schema v4 addendum): (1) C1.4's premise is literal: the majority need not include the grantor, and the requester need not reach it (Rule L3); (2) a `Grant` record is a grant; a resend of a recorded grant is a retransmission and records nothing new (schema v4); (3) a client stays a requester until it receives a grant answering its request, even if the request or grant was lost (Rule L3); (6) `quiescent` covers reads (E1 extended); (10) a message's fate is fixed at send; (12) how to write "both followers" in a link list (L1).
- **Fixed in the model briefs**: (8) model authors name every fairness condition; the legitimacy rule above was added after the review.
- **Recorded as residues, not fixed in X2**, because they are contract-level or predate X2: (4) the pause rule assumes grants are exclusive, which no clause says; (5) `holder` after a lost grant, and no `restart: grantor` counterpart; (7) every storage clause is "only if", so a reject-everything storage complies; (9) `Fault` fields are not given in the contract; (11) "storage replica" in F1.1 with one storage process; (13) `token: stale` is defined from the grantor's global view. From the hold-out author's second review, of the fixed inputs (eight items), all recorded as residues except the bound (above): obtaining a grant is not an observable event on the client, so C1.4's conclusion is witnessed only indirectly (a later action using the token, or the side's liveness check); late delivery to a process that has crashed and restarted, or after a heal, is unspecified; "fate fixed at send" still sits beside "may be reordered", which reads as delay; retransmission of a predecessor incarnation's grant by a successor is unaddressed; a liveness claim lives in a schedule comment, not in a field; `holder` still resolves from a receipt nothing records; the failure model's changelog no longer names its source document. Also from the review: P23 and P29 say "the ack is lost", which is one branch of Rule T2, not guaranteed (inherited from P8 and P17); P22 and P28's mutations are expected to be killed trivially, since F2.2 has little bite on a fence.
The steward's count: six of the thirteen were defects the steward introduced or left in X2's inputs; the rest predate them. The review then found that the first draft's briefs told the two arms different things about an unmutated failure, which would have biased the result toward regeneration on the liveness pairs. That was the most consequential steward error in X2's preparation, caught before any author started.
