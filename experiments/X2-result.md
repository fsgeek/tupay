## The mechanism
All five hold-out failures in X2 (A model H8 and H9, A impl H8 and H9, R impl H9) are one mechanism, named in all three judge verdicts (`experiments/X2/judge/`): **a leader that keeps its lease-node quorum but cannot exchange messages with the requester, while nothing relays the request through followers and nothing replaces the leader.** CheckQuorum keeps the leader; followers that hear it never elect; C1.4's premise holds through the followers (Rule L3). It is neither M1's D1 nor X1's D4.
- **A-H8** (explained, genuine violation, locus both): a simplex drop grantor→requester. Both sides decline to record a `Grant` they cannot deliver, so the hold-out's intended crash never fires; the premise already holds after the earlier fault. The judge's own `what_would_settle_it` leaves one door open: if a grant that cannot be sent is not "issued" and an unreached fault step changes the premise, the reading of H8 changes (see residues).
- **A-H9** (explained, genuine violation, locus both): a both-ways cut grantor↔requester.
- **R-H9** (explained, genuine violation, locus implementation): the regenerated model passes because its `Reaches` relays through a follower over up links (note 3); the regenerated implementation retries every lease node, but only the leader grants.
The arm A packets included the arm A model's README section by the stated excerpt rule, and both A verdicts cite its interpretation X2.5; the verdicts also rest on TLC states and trace records. The three verdicts are therefore not fully independent of the authors' own diagnosis.

## The thesis's falsifier
The log registered on 2026-10-21 what would contradict the thesis that independently derived artifacts, reconciled, raise robustness: "a round in which both sides agree on every pair and a held-out fault later shows they were wrong in the same way." **Arm A has that shape.** Both sides passed all thirty-one shared schedules, killed all thirty mutations, agreed on every hold-out, and failed H8 and H9 by the same mechanism.
Three facts bound what that shows:
1. **It was declared, not blind, on one side.** The arm A model author wrote down the exact failing shape before any hold-out existed (X2.5: "the model would print FAIL C1_4 on a schedule in which the requester is cut only from a grantor that keeps a majority … no shared schedule is like that"). Nothing in the workflow consumed that declaration: no step turns an author's stated limitation into a schedule, a question to the other side, or a prompt to the steward. The steward read it in the report and did not act on it before the hold-outs ran.
2. **The two sides of arm A were not cleanly independent.** Both inherited three rounds of reconciled history against the same steward-written interfaces, and both were given the same twelve steward-written schedules, none of which exercises a requester cut from a leader that keeps its quorum. A common cause in the inputs is at least as likely as a common cause in the authors.
3. **The independent arm diverged.** Arm R's model (same family as arm A's) relayed; its implementation (same family as arm A's) did not. The divergence pointed straight at the mechanism.
The inference, stated as one: the failing design (clients talk to the leader) is the default for leader-based services, and two artifacts derived against inputs that never exercise its edge share it whether or not their authors differ. The evidence for it is this one round. What the round does show: pairing alone did not find the defect; a third author's hold-outs did; and the one author who saw it could say so without the workflow hearing it. The thesis survives in a weaker form in which the hold-out author is part of the workflow, and the strong form ("disagreement between independently derived artifacts finds the defects") is contradicted for this round.

## Residues for the next round
- **C1.4 and request routing.** Rule L3 makes routing the artifact's business, and three of four sides do not route. Whether C1.4 should promise it, or the premise should require the requester to reach a node that can grant, is a contract question (contract v3, or X3), not an artifact fix.
- **Declared limitations go nowhere.** An author's stated limitation (X2.5) should become a question for the steward, a candidate schedule, or a prompt to the other side. Today it is prose in a README.
- **`Grant` when the reply link is down.** Both arm A sides decline to record a grant they cannot deliver; the schema says a grant is recorded by the process that produces it. Whether a grant that cannot be sent has been "issued" is underdetermined, and H8's reading depends on it.
- **Event `end`.** The regenerated model's compiler supports only `end: quiescent`; Rule E1 allows an event. Shared schedules should include one.
- **Unseen schedules.** Briefs should say the steward may run schedules the author has not seen.
- **Measure 6's soft spot.** Both rater pairs split the same way on "implementing exactly the shape the format prescribes" (kappa 0.64 on X1, 0.72 on X2). The definition needs a ruling before X3.
- **Measure 7's raters should not see the author's labels.** Give them the old README and the code diff, not the new README.
- **Hook coverage.** Include `scripts/hooks/` in every include-list checkout; add "a test commit gets stamped" to the input check.
