## 1. HIGH. Six of the twelve new schedules are liveness tests in disguise, and the arms are not told the same thing about what to do when liveness fails

**The dependency.** P25 and P26 are the registered liveness pairs. But P19 (steps 3 to 5), P20 (steps 3 to 5), P21 (step 5), P27 (step 5), and also P25 and P26 themselves, each require a `Grant` to be issued *while the F2.x fault is in force* before the schedule can proceed. Concretely:

- `P19.yaml:11-13`: `request: c2` and then `request: c1` must both be granted under `cut [[grantor, non-grantor]]`; the heal is triggered by `Grant c1`. P19 is therefore P25 plus a second grant under the same partial partition.
- `P20.yaml:11-13`: same shape under `drop [[n1, grantor], [n2, grantor], [n3, grantor]]`; P20 is P26 plus a second grant.
- `P21.yaml:12-13` and `P27.yaml:12-13`: `request: c2` must be granted while the grantor is cut from both followers but still reachable by c2. That is a routing requirement the v1 set never imposed: in P11 the requester sat on the majority side and could not reach the old grantor at all; here c2 can reach a deposed leader that cannot commit and must find the one that can. This is H9's mechanism in a weaker form, sitting in the shared set.

The mechanisms that can stall these are well known: dueling leaders across a partial partition (P19, P25: the cut-off follower never hears heartbeats, keeps raising its term through the middle node), a leader that is heard but cannot hear (P20, P26: followers never time out because they hear heartbeats; the leader never commits unless it checks its quorum), and a stale reachable leader (P21, P27).

**Consequences for scoring.**
(a) One stall mechanism will count two to four times in measure 1(a) (P25+P19, P26+P20, P21+P27), and each dependent pair's mutation becomes unassessable in 1(b). The carve-out at `X2-prereg.md:29` ("a liveness pair whose unmutated half already fails is counted as not killed") covers P25/P26 only. "Adaptation wins" requires A at least as correct on every item; a single stall in one arm therefore swings 1(a) by up to four and 1(b) by up to four, which is most of the twelve-pair delta. That is not wrong, but the pre-registration should say that it is one mechanism, not four defects, before the numbers exist; otherwise the result reads as "A failed 4, R failed 1" when the truth is "A has one stall class".
(b) `bound: {events: 200}` under dueling elections: the format does not say what hitting the bound means on either side. On P25/P26 a bound-hit before the grant is ambiguous between FAIL C1.4, UNEXPLAINED, and PASS-by-truncation. Define it before the run.

**The arms are told different things.** `x2-adapt-model.md:24` and `x2-adapt-impl.md:24`: "If the unmutated model fails a shared schedule, that is a result: record it, keep the counterexample, and do not change the model to make it pass unless you can say which clause or cell the old behaviour misread." The regen briefs have no such sentence; instead `x2-regen-model.md:20` and `x2-regen-impl.md:20` say `run.sh --mutations` "must report every one as FAIL", and `pairs-v3.md:39` says a liveness pair whose unmutated half fails has no working half to mutate. A regen author who sees P26 stall is pushed by its brief toward making it pass (model: add fairness; impl: add check-quorum) so that the mutation can "FAIL"; an adapt author is licensed to record the stall. This asymmetry biases 1(a)/1(b) toward R on exactly the item most likely to decide X2, and it is the adapt arm's honesty that would be penalised. Fix: put the same sentence in all four briefs, and soften "must" to "every mutation is expected to FAIL; if its unmutated half fails, say so and do not mutate it".

**Model-side fairness is the author's to choose, and it decides P25/P26.** `x2-adapt-model.md:26` and `x2-regen-model.md:32` ask authors to name fairness conditions and justify them. Good, but there is no pre-registered rule for which fairness assumptions are *legitimate*. A model author can put weak fairness on "leader commits the grant" and P26 passes by abstraction; the hold-out author warned of exactly this for H9 (`NOTES-fable.md:69`). Without a rule, 1(a) on the model side for the liveness pairs is at the author's discretion and X2-3's second clause ("model sides fail at least as often") is scored against whatever fairness each author happened to pick. A crisp rule that can be registered now: fairness may be assumed only on actions whose enablement depends on the acting process's local state and on delivery over links that are up; never on an action whose enablement requires having heard from a peer across a down link. Anything else is reported as a divergence with locus model, as D4 was.

**Recommendation.** Add to the pre-registration: (i) the dependency map above, with the rule "a stall is scored once as a mechanism in 1(e); dependent pairs are reported as *unreached*, not FAIL, and their mutations as *unassessable*"; (ii) the fairness legitimacy rule; (iii) bound-hit semantics; (iv) identical candour language in all four briefs. Optionally decouple P19/P20 from liveness by healing at `{event: Request, client: c1}` as P11 does (`P11.yaml:13`); the trade-off is that the second incarnation's grant then happens after the heal, which is P2's shape, so this is the steward's call.

## 3. HIGH. Measures 6 and 7 would not be scored the same way by two people, and the steward's own predictions hang on them

### Measure 6, propagation fraction (`X2-prereg.md:34`; prediction X2-7 at `:48`)
- **Unit.** "(commit, file) pair". A file with one conceptual hunk and five renamed fields is what? Say "conceptual if any hunk is conceptual" or classify by hunk. Also say whether README and predictions files are in the denominator, and whether the fraction is over pairs, commits, or lines.
- **Anchoring.** `x2-adapt-model.md:20` and `x2-adapt-impl.md:20` tell the author to label each commit "mechanical propagation or a change required by the meaning of F2.x". Measure 6 then says the raters classify "from the diffs alone". If the raters see commit messages, both are anchored by the author's self-label and the reported agreement is inflated. Say the messages are stripped before rating.
- **Definition.** "Required only because another element or interface changed shape" vs "required by the meaning of F2.x" does not settle the central case: adding per-direction link state to a transport is both. The log's original phrase (2026-10-21 entry, "mechanical updates for the new variables") suggests the operational test: *propagation* iff the change makes no choice the inputs leave open (a rename, a thread-through, a new field carried unchanged); *conceptual* iff it encodes a reading of F2.x or L3, or chooses between behaviours the inputs permit. Write that down.
- **Scoring.** X2-7 is scored on the steward's count. The steward predicted the number and will rate it; the second rater is reported but not scored. Pre-commit to scoring X2-7 on the second rater's count, or on the lower of the two.
- **Baseline.** X2-7's threshold 0.3 has no stated basis, and `:48` says the number is registered "mainly so that the number exists for X3". X1 arm A's commits exist and can be rated now, pre-outcome, giving a baseline that would make X2-7 a real comparison ("higher than X1's").

### Measure 7, interpretations contradicted (`X2-prereg.md:35`; prediction X2-5 at `:46`)
- **Denominator not stated.** Count the entries at `18f3c66` and `60e8f6e` now and write N per side into the pre-registration.
- **Classes not operational.** Suggested tests: *contradicted* iff the statement, as written, is false of the adapted artifact (there is a situation the old statement covered where the artifact now does otherwise); *extended* iff the statement remains true of every situation it covered and the artifact now also covers new ones; *unchanged* otherwise. Any rater can apply that; "by the steward from the adapted README and diff" cannot be applied by a second person.
- **Precedence rule injects bias.** "with the adapting author's own statements taking precedence where it makes one." The adapt briefs (`:16`) instruct the author to flag contradictions. ## 4. MEDIUM. Departure 1 changes what the cost half of the scoring rule measures, and the pre-registration does not say how it will discriminate

`X2-prereg.md:22` states the comparability break honestly. What it does not do is pre-register how a cost loss in arm A will be read. In X1 arm A's authors remembered their artifacts; in X2 arm A's authors must first read an artifact plus a README with (by X1's count) 49 or more interpretations before adapting. A's cost may exceed R's on the implementation side for that reason alone (the X1 margin there was 110k vs 127.5k). If it does, X2-1 is falsified, and the result will be narrated either as "the change was structural" or as "Departure 1", at the steward's discretion after the fact.

Pre-registerable discriminators:
- Split arm A's cost at the first adapting commit: tokens and wall-clock before it (reading, predicting) and after it (adapting). Both are observable from harness logs and commit timestamps. Score the standing prediction on *total* cost (the form adaptation takes in a non-persistent ayllu, as `:22` says) and report net-of-reading beside it; say which one X2-1 is scored on, now.
- For X2-6 (`:47`): the caveat already says confirmation is weak. Add the control that makes it informative: on a side where arm A contradicts *no* interpretation, if A/R still exceeds X1's fraction, Departure 1 explains the rise and X2-6's confirmation on the other side is null. Without the control X2-6 is close to unfalsifiable in practice (the fraction will rise) and vacuous if no side contradicts anything.
- State explicitly that "mixed" (`:38`) scores X2-1 as *not confirmed*. The X1 rule implies it; X2 will very likely produce a mixed table, so say it.
- "author cost (3) is lower" (`:38`): measure 3 lists five things. X1 scored tokens and wall-clock both. Say "tokens and wall-clock, both, per side".
