# Hold-out notes (H11–H14)

Schedules are in the checkout root. They use the v5 schedule shape. Shared pairs already cross C3.1, C3.4, C3.5, C3.7, C3.8, C1.1, C1.2, and C1.4 with F3.1–F3.3. These four aim at the crossings that list leaves out, and at v3 rules no pair states: invalid-before-busy, the storage decision rule across a membership change, an amnesic incumbent that still counts in the denominator, and a same-membership commit while the grantor can send and cannot hear.

## H11

**Targets:** C3.4, F1.2, F3.1, F3.2.

**Sequence:** At the start, n1 and the operator are partitioned from everyone else, so n1 has no path to n2 or n3. The operator sends r1, members `[n1, n2, n3]`, to n1. When n1 has recorded that request, the operator sends r2, members `[n1, s]`, to n1. Storage is not a lease node, so r2 is invalid. The schedule waits until r2 is refused `invalid`, then heals every link. The heal is not a fault. The next step steps n1's clock forward by one lifetime. That clock step is the last fault. The run then waits until it is quiescent.

**Liveness premise:** C3.4(a) applies to r2 as soon as n1 records it: n1 stays up, and the duty has no other premise. C3.4(b) promises nothing during the partition, because n1 is not connected to a majority of epoch 0. After the clock step, r1 is still pending at n1, every link is up, all three members are eligible (incarnation 0), and n1, a majority of epoch 0, and a majority of `[n1, n2, n3]` stay connected for the rest of the run. The operator stays connected to n1. C3.4(b) therefore requires r1 to commit, and requires the operator to be told. C1.4 promises nothing: no client is a requester.

**What a correct artifact must do:** Refuse r2 as `invalid` when it is recorded, including when r1 is already pending at n1. Checking busy first is a violation. Leave r1 pending through that refusal: the earlier request is never refused on account of the later one, and `invalid`, `busy`, and `superseded` are the only refusal reasons. Emit `ReconfigRefused` for r2 with `reason: invalid` and `epoch: null`, and, because the operator stays connected to n1, `ReconfigAck` for r2 with `outcome: refused`, caused by that refusal. After the clock step, commit epoch 1 with members exactly `{n1, n2, n3}`, `request_id: r1`, and `readmits` empty. The deciding `ConfigCommitted` carries `decided: true` and is counted by an eligible majority of epoch 0 and an eligible majority of those members (C3.8, C3.1). n1 records the outcome and the operator observes `ReconfigAck` for r1 with `outcome: committed`, caused by a `ConfigCommitted` that agrees in epoch, members, and request id with the decided record. Which eligible member decides, and which term it uses, are the protocol's choice. The clock reading is an input only if that decision consults n1's clock.

**Mistake it is meant to catch:** No shared pair sends an invalid set, and none leaves a valid request pending at a receiver who cannot see a majority. P40 partitions one follower and keeps both majorities connected, so the request can finish without a `busy` or `invalid` path. A careful team can check "am I already reconfiguring?" before it inspects the set, and refuse r2 as `busy`. It can also drop non-lease names and treat r2 as a commit of `{n1}`, or answer r1 with a reason outside the three (a quorum timeout is the ordinary one) because the liveness premise is false during the partition. The contract forbids that answer whenever the receiver is still holding the request. A one-node commit of r1 while the partition holds fails C3.8, because one agreement is not a majority of three.

**P(at least one of two careful teams fails it):** 0.55. The closed refusal list is easy to read as the list of liveness outcomes, and a stuck receiver then grows an `unavailable` timeout that no shared schedule forces into the trace.

## H12

**Targets:** C1.2, C2.1, C2.2, F3.1, F3.2.

**Sequence:** c1 obtains a grant under epoch 0 and writes `v1`. That write is accepted. Storage's clock then jumps forward by five lifetimes (the lifetime is one tick). c1 reads with the same token and, after that read is served, writes `v2` with the same token. On acceptance, the operator asks the grantor to commit members `[n1, n2, n4]` (n4 was outside epoch 0; n3 is removed). When that commit is decided, c2 requests a grant, reads, and writes `v3`. c1 then reads with the token it still holds, and c2 reads once more. Acks after c2's grant are not filtered on outcome. The run waits until it is quiescent. There is no partition. The storage clock step is the only fault, and it is the last fault.

**Liveness premise:** After the clock step every process stays up and connected, and every member that can be counted is incarnation 0. While r1 is pending, C1.4's premise is false. C3.4(b) holds for r1: the receiver is the grantor, a majority of `{n1, n2, n3}` and a majority of `{n1, n2, n4}` are eligible and stay connected to the receiver, and the operator stays connected to the receiver, so r1 commits and the operator is told. After that commit, the highest committed configuration is epoch 1. A majority of it is eligible and stays connected to storage and to c2, and no reconfiguration is pending. C1.4 requires c2 to obtain a grant.

**What a correct artifact must do:** Accept c1's write of `v1` (the ledger was 0). After the clock jump, serve c1's read of that same token and return `v1`. Accept c1's write of `v2` with that same token and store `v2`. The storage rule is token against ledger only; storage's clock is not an input to a correct storage decision. Commit epoch 1 with members exactly `{n1, n2, n4}` and `request_id: r1`, on eligible majorities of epoch 0 and of the new set, with `readmits` empty (every incarnation here is 0). c2's grant is issued under the deciding node's current configuration and is counted by an eligible majority of that configuration (C3.7). Which node decides, and the concrete token and term, are the protocol's choice. A decider that has recorded epoch 1 names epoch 1, and that token is then greater than every epoch-0 grant, including c1's (C1.1). A decider that has not recorded the commit can grant under epoch 0 with an eligible majority of `{n1, n2, n3}`; that token need not be greater than c1's, because "previously" is (a) uniqueness, (b) the epoch rule, and (c) one node's own counted order. After the `v2` write the ledger equals c1's token. c2's read is served with `v2` if and only if c2's token is at least that ledger, and rejected otherwise. c2's write of `v3` is accepted, and replaces the stored value, if and only if its token is at least the ledger. c1's following read and c2's final read obey the same rule against the ledger left by that write. A served read does not change the ledger. When c2's write was accepted, c1's token is below the ledger, so c1's read is rejected and c2's final read is served with `v3`. When c2's write was rejected, c2's token is below c1's, so c1's read is served with `v2` and c2's final read is rejected. Every accepted `WriteAck` and every served `ReadAck` names a storage decision that already happened, and the value given is the value that decision served (C1.3, C2.2).

**Mistake it is meant to catch:** P49 fences a stale write across a reconfiguration. P13–P15 and P27–P28 fence stale reads without a membership change and without an equal token. P50 steps the holder's clock, then checks that a stale write is rejected, which is the outcome a storage clock check would also produce. None of those require a non-stale read to succeed after storage's own clock is past the lifetime, none require a second write with the same token, and none require the new configuration's holder to be served the value written under the old one. A careful team can implement reads as `token > ledger`, reject an equal token, or treat lease expiry as storage's job. It can also keep the value in the old leader's state and serve `null`, or reject, once epoch 1 is committed, until a write happens under the new epoch.

**P(at least one of two careful teams fails it):** 0.5. Equal-token reads and writes are the case "at least the ledger" adds, and every shared fencing pair uses a strictly older token, so `>` on the read path still passes them.

## H13

**Targets:** C3.7, C1.4, C1.2, F1.1, F1.2, F3.2, F3.3.

**Sequence:** Five lease nodes start in epoch 0. c1 obtains a grant. n1 crashes and restarts amnesic, then n2 crashes and restarts amnesic. Neither is re-admitted. The schedule then partitions `{n1, n2, n3, n4, c2}` from the rest, so the other side holds n5, c1, storage, and the operator. c2 requests a grant on the side that contains exactly two eligible members, n3 and n4. c1 writes `v1` to storage from the other side. When that write is acknowledged, every link is healed. Storage's clock then steps forward by one lifetime. That step is the last fault. The run waits until it is quiescent.

**Liveness premise:** The partition is not the last fault, and during it neither side holds an eligible majority of the five, so C1.4 promises c2 nothing in that window. c1's write is a storage decision and does not need the liveness premise. After the clock step the links are up for the rest of the run. Eligible members are n3, n4, and n5 (three of five; n1 and n2 remain in the denominator and are ineligible). That is more than half. They stay connected to each other, to storage, and to c2, and no reconfiguration is pending. C1.4 requires c2 to obtain a grant. C3.4 promises nothing: no reconfiguration is sent.

**What a correct artifact must do:** Accept c1's write of `v1`. The ledger was 0 and the token is a positive grant token; n1 and n2 being amnesic does not change storage. Issue c2 a grant only on eligible agreements from a majority of epoch 0, which means at least three of the five, none of them incarnation 1 of n1 or n2. A grant counted by n3 and n4 alone, or by either amnesic incarnation, fails C3.7. After the clock step, issue that grant and deliver it to c2. Its token is unique and respects C1.1 against c1's grant; the integer and the term are the protocol's choice. The deciding node's current configuration is epoch 0, because nothing commits a later epoch. `readmits` does not arise. Storage's one-lifetime step is an input only to a decision that consults storage's clock; a correct grant does not.

**Mistake it is meant to catch:** P54 and P60 leave the surviving eligible members connected to each other, so a grant can be counted without the amnesic node, and shrinking the denominator from 3 to 2 still demands two votes. Here any grant c2 obtains during the partition can include at most n3 and n4 from the eligible set. Counting either new incarnation, or dropping the amnesic members out of the denominator so that two votes carry a five-node configuration, is what makes that grant possible. Shared schedules never put exactly two eligible members on the side where the client is asking. A careful team can follow the usual "replaced disk leaves the quorum" pattern, which is the opposite of this denominator, and still pass P54.

**P(at least one of two careful teams fails it):** 0.6. Keeping an amnesic member in every denominator until a commit omits it is the rule most careful Raft-style implementations get backwards, and no shared schedule makes two votes look like a majority only after that mistake.

## H14

**Targets:** C3.4, C3.8, C3.1, F2.2, F3.1.

**Sequence:** c1 obtains a grant, which fixes `grantor`. The schedule then drops every lease node's link to that grantor. A pair that names the grantor twice is ignored, so the grantor hears nothing from n1, n2, or n3, while its own messages to them are still delivered. Follower-to-follower links stay up. The operator sends r1, members `[n1, n2, n3]`, to `non-grantor`. The drop is never healed. The run ends when the operator records `ReconfigAck` with `outcome: committed`.

**Liveness premise:** The drop is the last fault. For the rest of the run the receiver is a non-grantor, the other non-grantor is connected to it in both directions, and those two are an eligible majority of epoch 0 and of the requested set `{n1, n2, n3}` (every incarnation is 0). The operator stays connected to the receiver. C3.4(b) requires r1 to be committed, or refused as `superseded`, and requires the operator to be told. Nothing else can occupy epoch 1, so the outcome that meets the clause is commit. The grantor itself stays disconnected and is not part of the premise. C1.4 promises nothing new: c1's request was answered before the drop, and no later request is sent. C3.4(a) does not apply: the set is non-empty and names only lease nodes, and no earlier request is pending.

**What a correct artifact must do:** Commit epoch 1 with members exactly `{n1, n2, n3}`, `request_id: r1`, and `readmits` empty. The decided record is counted by an eligible majority of epoch 0 and an eligible majority of the same set; one agreement from a node in both carries `counted_toward: [0, 1]`. At most one configuration is committed for epoch 1, and epoch 0 is already committed (C3.1). The receiver records the outcome and answers the operator. `ReconfigAck` carries `outcome: committed` and is caused by a `ConfigCommitted` that agrees in epoch, members, and request id with that decided record. The operator is told success only for r1, and only after the commit. A same-membership request is a real commit: it is how a later re-admission would be requested, and this run simply has an empty `readmits`. Which of the two non-grantors decides is the protocol's choice on the model side and the lowest-numbered non-grantor on the implementation side. The deaf grantor must not produce a second decided configuration for epoch 1.

**Mistake it is meant to catch:** P26 is this simplex for a client grant. P40 is a reconfiguration under a partition that still leaves the receiver a majority, with a changed member set. No pair asks for the same members, and no pair asks for a commit while the current grantor can still send and cannot hear. A careful team can special-case client retries so P26 passes, and still forward a reconfiguration to the leader they can hear, who will never collect a majority. A careful team can also treat "members unchanged" as a no-op and leave r1 pending, or refuse it with a reason other than the three. Either way the committed ack never arrives. A deaf grantor that counts its own unacknowledged proposal as a commit can also write a second epoch-1 configuration.

**P(at least one of two careful teams fails it):** 0.55. The adaptation that makes a grant progress under F2.2 is easy to hang off the client path, and the same-membership commit has no shared pair that would force it to be a real epoch.

## Input defects

1. **A served read both does and does not fence the older token.** Under "How v1 and v2 read under v3", the deterministic rule says storage serves a read if and only if its token is at least the ledger, and C2.1 says: "Serving a read does not change the highest accepted token." The next bullet says: "storage rejects the older holder only once a write or read with a newer token has been accepted or served." A newer read that does not move the ledger leaves the older token greater than or equal to the ledger, so the deterministic rule still serves it. A newer read that fences the older token has to move the fence the deterministic rule says a read does not move. One artifact cannot satisfy both sentences. H12 fences only with a write, which both sentences agree about. The schedules do not require a particular result for a stale read that follows a newer read and precedes any newer write.

2. **Who must be told about an `invalid` or `busy` refusal.** C3.4 says the receiver refuses in (a), then in (b) says the request is "eventually committed or refused as `superseded`", and then: "In either case, if the operator also stays connected to the receiver, it is eventually told which (`ReconfigAck`)." "Either case" reads most naturally as the two outcomes in (b), so a contract-only reading can omit `ReconfigAck` for `invalid` and `busy`. The v5 L3 extension says the opposite in plainer words: "C3.4(a) needs only that the receiver stays up; either duty to tell the operator holds if the operator also stays connected to the receiver." H11 follows L3 and treats the ack as required. The two texts disagree, and L3 is the one a schedule compiler is told to implement.
